Since 2015rooted in La Chocita del Valle

A project started by families

The kindergarten grew out of La Chocita del Valle, a project that in 2015 brought together a group of families from Ávila who wanted a respectful education for their children.

They chose Waldorf education, an approach based on the work of Rudolf Steiner in which teachers and families are involved in equal measure. Today Ingrid and Aldara accompany the group every morning.

Our approach

Five ideas behind every morning

01 / 05

Free play and imitation

Free play is a child’s natural activity and the heart of the day. Toys are simple, made of wood and cloth, so imagination does the rest. The adult is the model children imitate in gesture, speech and the way they treat others.

02 / 05

A warm, homely atmosphere

Children of different ages share one group, as in a family. The older ones look after the younger, the younger imitate the older, and a sense of community and cooperation grows on its own.

03 / 05

Rhythm and artistic activity

The day is like one long breath: moments of expansion and moments of gathering in, taking turns. Watercolour, bread, beeswax and wool carry real educational value and come around every week.

04 / 05

Nature every day

Vegetable patch, soil, puddles and seasons. Direct contact with nature gives children a whole world of learning and health, whatever the weather.

05 / 05

Families as active partners

Families are not spectators: they take part in the life of the kindergarten, in the seasonal festivals, in the talks on education and in the workshops.

A day in the kindergarten

A morning that breathes

An indicative Waldorf morning. Each group adjusts it to its age and to the season.

  1. Arrival

    Free play

    Each child arrives at their own pace and slips into play. The teachers are present, busy with real work: sewing, kneading, tidying.

  2. Circle

    Songs and verses

    We gather in a circle for songs, verses and finger games that change with the seasons.

  3. Breakfast

    Together at the table

    We prepare and share breakfast. One day we bake bread; another, we cut fruit.

  4. Outdoors

    In the yard

    Vegetable patch, sand, puddles and trees. We go out in any weather, well wrapped up.

  5. Story

    Gathering in

    A story told, not read, repeated over several days so the children can live it from the inside.

  6. Goodbye

    With the families

    A farewell verse and home, with time to talk to the teachers.

Waldorf versus traditional preschool

What changes, and what the child gains

This is not about being against anyone. It is about respecting how a young child learns: with the body, through play, by imitating and repeating. These are the differences families notice most.

Traditional preschoolWaldorf kindergartenWhat the child gains
Worksheets, letters and numbers from age 3Free play as the central activityInitiative, creativity and the ability to solve problems alone
Plastic toys, lights and screensWood, wool, cloth and natural materialsAwake senses and an imagination that fills in what the toy leaves out
A timetable broken into activitiesA steady daily and seasonal rhythmSecurity, calm and the ability to self-regulate
Classes split by ageOne mixed-age groupCooperation, care for the little ones and learning from peers
Short playtime, only in good weatherOutdoors every day, in any weatherHealth, resilience, gross motor skills and confidence in their own body
Occasional craftsWatercolour, bread, beeswax, wool and music every weekFine motor skills, sustained attention and artistic sensitivity
Adult-led instructionThe adult as a model to imitateLearning by experience, without pressure or assessment
Early reading and writingReading and writing when the child is ready, around 6 or 7No early anxiety; comparative studies find no lasting advantage in starting early

What a Waldorf child takes with them

Confidence

They can make things with their hands and their body, and they know it.

Creativity

They turn a piece of cloth into a house, a boat or a cape.

Attention

They have learned to concentrate on what they are doing, not to wait for stimulation.

Social sense

They have cared for the little ones and been cared for by the older ones.

Love of nature

They know the soil, the rain and the cold of Ávila first hand.

Eagerness to learn

They reach primary school with their curiosity intact and in no hurry.

Practical information

What families usually ask

Which ages do you take?

Children from 18 months to 5 years, in one mixed-age group.

What are the hours?

Mornings. Write to us for the exact drop-off and pick-up times of the current school year.

Do I need to know Waldorf education already?

No. We run talks and open workshops throughout the year, and we will be happy to explain it to you.

How do we enrol?

Write or call us and we will arrange a visit so you can see the space and meet the teachers.

Do you go outside when it rains or snows?

Yes. In waterproofs and well wrapped up, the yard and the puddles are part of the day.
